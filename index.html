Primera Suite

A personal suite of open source color grading DCTLs (DaVinci Resolve Studio required) for primary balancing and some aspects of look development.

Primera consolidates the grading approaches I reach for most often in one place, under one name, and with only the controls I actually use. Most of the underlying math comes from publicly-available, tried-and-tested imaging science and from extensive use of open-source DCTLs provided by the generous members of the "color-concerned community."

My personal aesthetic lodestar remains a film-like look, in the broad sense, however not by emulating any specific stock or process (with one sort-of exception) and in any case the tools should be flexible enough to achieve a broad range of looks.

The DCTLs are meant to be used in the context of a fully color-managed workflow. They've been used and tested most against OpenDRT, but play well with Resolve's CST, 2499 DRT, ACES 2.0, and correctly-constructed 3D LUTs.

Primary Grading

Primera

Primera.dctl

The foundation. Provides primary grading controls for shot-to-shot balancing across the range of source and working space encodings I encounter most often: ARRI LogC3 and LogC4, Sony S-Log3, REDLog3G10, ACEScct, DaVinci Intermediate, and Kodak Cineon.

  • Exposure Stops — Linear gain in photographic stops (2n) applied before the selected transfer function
  • Black Point — Smooth compression of the darkest tones approaching black via subtraction of a constant (called "flare" in Baselight)
  • Temp / Tint — White balance operating in linear space
  • Contrast — Log-space tonal stretch/squash around a variable Pivot defaulted to the selected transfer function's mid-grey point
  • Shadows / Highlights — Linear gain constrained to operate below/above mid-grey; no spatial operations so may be encoded in a 3D LUT
  • Roll Off — tanh highlight compression; works with Highlights to control the shoulder's curve shape
  • Neg. Saturation — Multiplicative negative gain on chroma
  • Pos. Saturation — HSV-based "density" boost
  • Preserve Luma - Sat — Both Saturation controls will only darken the image, this option offsets (via positive linear gain) this consequence
  • Show Chart — Per-transfer-function stop-graduated step chart, à la the chart shown here by Walter Volpatto

Hue & Density

Primera Hue

PrimeraHue.dctl

Per-channel hue and density control via tetrahedral interpolation, based on hotgluebanjo's DCTL implementation of an approach described by Steve Yedlin ASC in his DisplayPrep demo and follow-up discussion from 2018.

  • 6 Hue sliders (R/Y/G/C/B/M) — Rodrigues' rotation around each corner's achromatic axis; +/−60° per channel covers the full 360° disc in HSV
  • 6 Density sliders — Makes the shifted color subjectively more colorful without adding energy
  • Soft / Hard Clamp — tanh shoulder + exponential toe compression, or hard clip to [0,1]; both can be applied or just one
  • Cinecolor — Emulates a largely forgotten "budget" two-strip color process in use from about the late 1930s to the early 1950s. Similar to Technicolor Process 2, Cinecolor used bi-packed ortho negatives and duplitized print stock (emulsion on both sides of the base) and now it's here for you in 2026 only without the registration nightmares
  • Protect Skintones — Applies only to Cinecolor; creates a holdout matte centered on ~28° HSV with smooth falloff

Split Toning

Primera Split

PrimeraSplit.dctl

Subtractive color split-toning for imbuing shadows and highlights with (usually) contrasting color casts. Lately conceptualized less as an effect and more as a fundamental look development tool, defining the chroma vectors of the characteristic curve.

  • Mid-Grey Pivot — Defaults to the selected transfer function's mid-grey but best set by eye
  • Transition Softness — Controls the blend width between shadow and highlight zones
  • Preserve Luminance — As in Primera and PrimeraHue
  • Ramp Position — Positions the greyscale ramp vertically; useful when working under blanking
  • Show Curve / Show Pivot / Show Chart — Diagnostic overlays for curve shape, pivot position and transition softness, and the same per-TF stop chart as in Primera

Skintone Sculpting

Primera Skin

PrimeraSkin.dctl

A dedicated skintone sculpting tool operating in HSV, meant for log-based sources/groups/timelines. Targets a "fuzzy pie slice" of the HSV disc centered on the nominal skin tone hue angle (~28°) and applies adjustments within that region only. Everything outside the mask passes through untouched. Gamut containment via the same tanh / exponential soft squeeze used throughout.

  • Hue — Rotates skin hue (+/−20°)
  • Saturation — Scales HSV saturation within the mask
  • Density — Adjusts value/brightness (positive = darker)
  • Range — Widens or narrows the skin mask (0.25 = tight, 2.0 = broad)
  • Evenness — Compresses hues toward the skin center; emulates HMU evening-out talent skintones on set
  • Low / High Gate — Value-based gates excluding dark or bright pixels from the mask
  • Show Mask — Three-zone false-color overlay shows the "skin zone" in yellow/gold while magenta denotes the pixels fall outside of the skin vector in the clockwise direction and green too far counter-clockwise. Usable with most trasnfer functions but best observed in Log space.
  • Legend — shows a key for three zones in a gradient strip across the bottom of the viewer

Notes

  • The math underlying the Primera tools is available in both Typst source and PDF. Feedback welcome.
  • These tools are designed to be used in the context of a fully color-managed workflow. See here for a primer if you're unfamiliar with this approach.
  • It is possible to produce negative or otherwise out-of-range values despite the guardrails/containment in place. I usually reach for gamut compression first if this happens but if something's obviously broken, please file an issue.
  • Kaur Hendriksen's free standalone DCTL implements ACES 2.0 gamut compression coefficients and is heartily recommended. Resolve's ACES Transform or CST also (usually) work.
